Summary

First edition, published November 2, 2026. The fourth round of observation, following issue #000 (July 2026), #001 (August 2026) and #002 (September 2026). This round used gpt-6.

In issue #002, the share of referral-type sources — comparison media and OTAs, which earn referral commissions — had partially receded from the issue #001 spike. This round, the direction split by category. On the 4-language average, kimono fell from 55.5% to 51.3%, while tea ceremony rose from 32.6% to 53.8% and zen meditation from 6.1% to 21.9%. Zen had stayed in the single digits for three rounds; this is its first move.

Cell by cell, 7 of the 12 cells moved beyond the noise-floor threshold relative to issue #002: 6 up, 1 down (kimono/Japanese, from 78.4% to 26.2%).

The make-up of citations changed too. Citations per answer rose from 2.8 to 6.5. The public-sector share fell from 35% to 9%, while owned sites rose from 30% to 43% and OTAs from 2% to 10%. Between issues #002 and #003, two things changed at once: the model (GPT-5.6 Sol → gpt-6) and the way citations are captured. The differences reported here cannot be attributed to either one, and no causal claim is made.

Among the 34 businesses fixed in advance, the top positions were largely kept (top-5 overlap between consecutive issues: 3.1, 3.1 and 3.9 out of 5). Looking beyond the fixed set — at the businesses' own-site domains — new top-5 domains appeared in all 12 cells (overlap with issue #002: 1.9 out of 5 on average). "The recommended shops are stable overall" cannot be written from this issue's data, and neither can the opposite.

In 56 of 240 answers (23.3%), the shop recommended in the text and the shop shown on the map card did not match; another 10 answers showed only landmarks on the map (66 in total, 27.5%). This counting rule was fixed after the observation, so the figure is provisional.

Method

Runs used ChatGPT (gpt-6, web version) in a temporary chat while logged in, on 2026-10-22 to 24, in Kita-ku, Kyoto, over a home fibre connection. Queries followed the usual keyword pattern in Japanese, English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese, for tea ceremony, kimono rental, and zen meditation; the 12 queries are not translations of each other but the forms speakers of each language would type. Each query was run 20 times (n=20) consecutively, 240 answers in all. Business names and cited domains were recorded; sources were classified into the same six types as previous issues — Public, Owned, OTA, Media, Directory, UGC.

This round's citation capture differs from earlier ones: it records the set of safe URLs attached to each response that carry the ChatGPT source marker, without the pairing between titles and URLs. The memory feature was off for the four zen cells and its state is unknown for the other eight. Exact query strings and conditions are in the Japanese full text, §2 and appendix.

Scope & limitations

The model and the citation-capture method changed together between issues #002 and #003, so no difference is attributed to either; the question of whether the lineup stays stable when the model does not change is deferred until two consecutive issues share a model. The noise-floor thresholds measure sampling error only. Memory state differs between categories (control sample n=5: no visible difference, low power). The 34 fixed businesses were chosen early and cannot show later entrants. The map-card counting rule was fixed after the observation. Five cells had interruptions or time gaps during observation. This study is limited to ChatGPT, a single observer, a single execution location, and recommendation-type queries. Full discussion in the Japanese full text, §7.
